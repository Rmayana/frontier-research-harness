# Iterative strategy research

Use this reference for whole-goal strategy research, interacting targets, or a
request to create hypotheses and experiments.

## Scientific stance

Begin with the strongest existing evidence and best-documented elite practice.
Form a revisable leading strategy and causal explanation for the whole goal. It
should connect interventions to immediate physiological or mechanical states,
adaptation, capacities, and whole-person performance; it is not a list of target
values.

Follow the approved whole-body goal; body and technique can inform each other
where combat is the application. Do not replace the goal with that application.
Keep the current synthesis in free-text `current_understanding` when useful;
its saved history supports reconsideration without a fixed research template.

Treat every source, observation, calculation, and model run as a new observation
about that explanation. Before reporting it, integrate its implications. Reason
forward to consequences for the goal, backward to the variables or mechanisms
needed to explain it, and across to interactions, costs, and alternative methods.
Calculate or bound newly inferable quantities. Let discrepancies, unexplained
links, surprising trade-offs, and promising combinations create the next
question naturally. If answering that question could change the strategy and a
qualified investigation is available, pursue it in the same research request.

This is natural scientific reasoning, not a stored sequence. Do not impose a
question score, fixed question or hypothesis count, mandatory null hypothesis,
phase, gate, or lifecycle object. Generate alternatives only when they are
scientifically credible and decision-relevant.

## Resolution and invention

Reverse-design from the desired whole-body outcome: ask which structures,
physiology, mechanisms, and feasible methods could produce it, then check their
consequences forward. Move freely between broad synthesis and organ, tissue,
cellular, molecular, or mathematical detail, and sideways across systems.
Let each result determine which scale or question is useful next. A local finding
may reshape the whole strategy; a whole-body conflict may call for a finer model.
There is no mandatory order, scale coverage, or questionnaire. Keep the links to
the goal, realization methods, and cross-target consequences intelligible.

Novel improvements should be mechanism-constrained extensions of strong
existing methods. Decompose why the best existing methods work, compare where
their useful mechanisms and costs differ, and derive a new combination or
condition only when the causal explanation supports it. Judge efficiency in the
terms that matter to the current goal, including adaptation, transfer, recovery
cost, risk, and interference. For candidate body configurations, use the user's
explicit equal-weight normalized objective and its recorded definitions. It does
not score research questions or replace causal interpretation and reverse design.

When a result provokes a quantitative question, use compatible established
relations, physical laws, inspected data, or qualified models to calculate it.
Simple arithmetic may be done directly; a more complex question may justify a
small temporary script, equation system, parameter sweep, statistical model, or
surrogate. Do not limit reasoning to a fixed target calculator and do not build a
permanent tool before repeated use demonstrates a need. Show the equations,
inputs, units, coefficient provenance, assumptions, outputs, sensitivity,
uncertainty, and validation route. If a causal bridge cannot be supported, mark
it unresolved rather than filling it with intuition.

## Experiments and digital twin

When a run fails, inspect its saved failure receipt. Distinguish software or
input errors from model limitations and evidence against the hypothesis. A
successful process exit alone does not validate the scientific explanation.
The next investigation follows from that diagnosis, not a required retry count.

Use the smallest qualified experiment that can change the decision. Prefer an
established external model within its validated endpoint, population, inputs,
and horizon. A digital-twin run may exchange state only between such qualified
models. Reference and population runs support conditional conclusions, not a
personal forecast.

Before execution, make the scientific contrast clear enough to interpret the
result. Preserve for every run:

- the exact question and compared scenarios;
- every supplied input, unit, constant, range, time, seed, and calibration
  label;
- model and runtime identity, version, hash, validation scope, and provenance;
- every raw output, uncertainty field, warning, failure, and unresolved value;
  and
- the inference and strategy change attributed to the result.

Use parameter sweeps or alternative scenarios when thresholds, reversals,
trade-offs, or non-identifiability matter. There is no mandatory sample count.
Independent pooled endpoints are not an interacting strategy simulation.

## Collaborative research

Substantial research may unfold as a live conversation. Surface a material
result together with the concise scientific rationale, derived quantities,
questions it genuinely raised, and any change to the leading strategy. Continue
safe in-scope investigation without waiting for permission; the user may inspect,
challenge assumptions, contribute observations, or redirect emphasis at any
time. Treat that feedback as additional research information, not as a substitute
for Codex generating the next scientific question.

## Stop and report

Continue in the same research request when another available investigation can
still distinguish live explanations or alter the strategy. Stop when qualified
evidence and models no longer distinguish them, deeper detail cannot change the
decision, or the next informative step requires personal measurement,
real-world experimentation, or new user authority.

Report the path that actually occurred: the evolving strategy, material results,
quantities derived from them, questions that emerged, investigations performed,
and resulting revisions. Do not manufacture questions after the fact or dump an
unrelated inventory. Separate facts, external evidence, observations,
model-bounded results, calculations, inference, hypotheses, and unknowns. End
with the current leading strategy, what changed it, surviving explanations,
important cross-target effects, unresolved causal links, and the next informative
experiment.
