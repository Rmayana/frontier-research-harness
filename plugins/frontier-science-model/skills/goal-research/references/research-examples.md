# Optional research examples

These fictional examples illustrate observable research judgments, not private
reasoning transcripts, scientific evidence, or required steps. Read only when an
example would clarify the requested behavior. Do not copy their assumptions into
the real goal or research database. Different valid paths are welcome.

## Missing measurement, useful conditional estimate

A user wants a machine to run twice as long, but its battery capacity is unknown.
Measured power consumption is available. The researcher calculates the energy
needed for the requested duration under explicit operating assumptions, then
asks whether reducing consumption or increasing capacity could satisfy that
requirement. The result is a conditional requirement, not a fabricated battery
measurement. Investigating duty cycle may matter more than finding an exact
capacity immediately; the researcher chooses based on the goal.

## An unexpected result changes the explanation

A supplied thermal model predicts that a larger heat sink should lower peak
temperature, but an experiment does not. The researcher checks execution and
conditions, then notices that the prediction assumed unrestricted airflow.
Airflow restriction becomes a competing explanation, with a testable prediction
under controlled flow. Merely reporting the discrepancy would leave the original
strategy unsupported; the revised explanation determines what to investigate.
If model scope or evidence cannot distinguish the alternatives, retain that
uncertainty rather than declaring a new mechanism established.

## A local improvement changes the whole strategy

A component becomes more efficient only at a higher operating temperature.
Another component's limit may make that gain unusable in the full system. The
researcher retrieves their original operating conditions and explores whether
isolation, another operating point, or a different design could preserve the
benefit. A promising integration is a new hypothesis until checked; separate
component successes do not establish simultaneous attainability.

## A calculation opens a different route

A calculation shows that even a perfect version of the proposed component cannot
meet the user's objective under the current architecture. Instead of indefinitely
optimizing it, the researcher asks why the architecture needs that component and
investigates another arrangement. This changes the proposed means, not the
approved goal. Record the assumptions behind the bound so new evidence can
challenge it later.
